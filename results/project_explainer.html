Nguasach · cross-linguistic phonosemantics

Does a word's sound tell you what it means?

Mostly not — that's Saussure's point. But at corpus scale a small systematic residue shows up: similar-sounding words tend to mean similar things. This is a rebuild of a 2021 hobby project into a reproducible pipeline that measures that residue across 22 languages, with the controls the original lacked.

01The question

Two ways to ask it

One a prediction task, one a correlation.

Retrieval. Learn a map between two languages' sound-spaces from 90 % of their translation pairs. Given a held-out word's sound alone, can the map retrieve its correct translation, better than a shuffled-label null?

Correlation (Mantel). Build the matrix of pairwise sound distances among concepts and the matrix of pairwise meaning distances. Do they line up — and do they still line up after subtracting out how similar the words are spelled (which absorbs cognates and borrowing)?

Headline claims use the four hand-verified language columns — English, Chinese, French, Irish. The other 18 (machine-translated, audited but not trusted cell-by-cell) are a labelled exploratory extension.

02The data

1,842 concepts × 22 languages

The concept list leans on verbs, adjectives and everyday nouns. Each concept is translated into every language, then turned into two vectors — one for how it sounds, one for what it means.

Concept → word nguasach.xlsx
One written form per language. English/Chinese/French/Irish checked by hand; the rest from Google Translate, with an offline QC pass that flags untranslated or copied cells (Swahili 15 %, Hungarian 8 %, …).
Word → IPA eSpeak NG 1.52 · pinyin map for Mandarin
Phonemic transcription; stress, length and tone stripped.
IPA → sound vector phonetic-similarity-vectors
Each word becomes a bag of articulatory feature-bigrams (voiced‑bilabial‑stop, …), then whitened PCA to 300 dimensions.
Concept → meaning vector word2vec → PCA-50
A 26k-word English word2vec model, reduced to 50 whitened dimensions.

03How it's measured

The map, and the controls it has to beat

The retrieval map is a ridge regression between the two sound-spaces — regularised, unlike the original's plain least squares, which overfits at ~18 training pairs per parameter. Scoring uses exact cosine retrieval with CSLS de-hubbing, 10-fold cross-validation, and a 1,000-permutation null that reshuffles the translation pairing and re-runs the entire fit. Everything gets Benjamini–Hochberg FDR.

Three non-learned baselines run through the identical machinery, so the learned map's contribution is isolable:

  • editdist — rank candidates by raw string similarity. The cognate control.
  • orth — character n-gram overlap. Cross-script pairs score at chance.
  • feat — mean articulatory-feature vector. Coarse, script-independent phonology.

The partial Mantel residualises both distance matrices on the orthographic edit-distance matrix before correlating — the step that decides whether "iconicity" is the right word or whether it's just shared spelling.

04What came out

A small real effect, concentrated in the oldest vocabulary

loanwords

Internationalisms (chocolate, tsunami, saxophone) dilute the signal rather than inflate it — they sound alike everywhere but mean unrelated things. Excluding 205 of them nudges the median correlation up.

verbs

Verbs are near zero overall, and the sign flips with morphology: significantly negative for the agglutinative languages (Japanese −0.043, Korean, Turkish, Russian). Regular conjugation endings make similar-sounding verbs share inflection, not meaning.

cross-family

Between unrelated languages the form ~ form correlation is real but minuscule (median partial r ≈ 0.01), present for ~80 % of pairs. Too weak for a universality claim; not nothing.

The phoneme–pole ("hexagram") analysis — which phonemes cluster with which meanings — yields nothing that survives FDR.

05The etym layer

Syllables as trigram compositions

The interpretability side carries a constructed system. Eighteen semantic poles — nine I‑Ching trigrams, each static (a spatial configuration) or dynamic (a motion) — stand for the primitives of a Language-of-Thought conlang. An etym (atom + etymology, after Finnegans Wake) is the smallest unit that fuses form, grounded meaning, and a cognitive operation, and sits at a consonant×vowel cell.

The pipeline builds the Mandarin onset×rhyme grid: for each syllable, it pools every concept whose Chinese word contains it, averages their meaning vectors, and reports which trigram pole — or which additive (parallel) pair of poles — that average lands nearest, plus the syllable's over-used phonemes and its example words.

Open the Etym Table → — a browsable onset×rhyme matrix; click any cell for its pole structure, phoneme skew, meaning neighbours, and word list.

06Reproducing it

One command, frozen configs

nguasach all --config configs/paper_confirmatory.yaml

runs data → translate-qc → ipa → phonetics → semantics → align → mantel → associate → baselines → etym → report.

  • Every stage writes a JSON run manifest — config hash, git SHA, input checksums, backend versions.
  • Frozen configs for the confirmatory (4 languages), exploratory (all 22), and loanword-excluded runs; results under results/paper_*/.
  • notebooks/figures.py regenerates every figure from results/.
  • Python 3.10, pinned; the vendored feature-vector code at commit 58f5639.
  • The original scripts and outputs are kept in legacy_src/ and legacy_results/.
